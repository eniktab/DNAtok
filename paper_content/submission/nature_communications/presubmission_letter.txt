Subject: Presubmission enquiry — Beyond NLP tokenization: GPU-native, bit-identical tokenizers for genomic foundation models


Dear Editor,

I am writing to ask whether the work I describe below might fit Nature Communications before I prepare a formal submission.

Significance (~60 words). Genomic foundation models inherited their tokenization from natural-language processing, where vocabularies of tens of thousands of subwords made sense. DNA has four letters. We re-engineer tokenization natively for the genomic alphabet across every major model family, producing bit-identical outputs while removing 60- to 2000-fold of inherited preprocessing overhead — letting GPU cycles go to the model itself, not to language-modelling machinery DNA never needed.

The concept. Inference cost remains the dominant wall-clock budget for genomic foundation models, and we do not change that. What we change is the tokenization layer that feeds those models — a layer that is, today, an artefact of language modelling. It assumes a vocabulary of tens of thousands of subwords learned over human text, runs Python loops over byte-pair-encoding (BPE) merge tables, and string-manipulates character-level inputs through interpreted bytecode. The structural assumptions that motivated this design in NLP do not transfer to a four-letter alphabet. As genomic models scale to whole-chromosome contexts and high-throughput pipelines, this inherited preprocessing tax becomes a meaningful and avoidable share of total runtime.

The contribution. I built a tokenization framework that natively targets DNA across every major foundation-model family currently in deployment: DNABERT-2 and GENA-LM (BPE), the Nucleotide Transformer series (NTv2, NTv3) at k-mer and character resolutions, METAGENE-1 (Llama-style, metagenomics), HyenaDNA, Caduceus, and Evo2 (character-level). For each model, the framework produces output bit-identical to the upstream reference tokenizer, so existing checkpoints work unchanged. Internally, the system replaces general-purpose NLP tokenization with GPU-native primitives tailored to DNA's small alphabet, including a parallel BPE kernel that preserves canonical leftmost-merge semantics while exploiting the structural simplicity of genomic vocabularies.

Evidence. We ran publication-grade benchmarking at 50 timing iterations and 2000 CDF iterations per cell (>=1000 measurements per platform), across three GPU generations and five workload regimes spanning short reads to 32-kbp ultra-long contexts. At standard inference workloads (batch 32, 1 kbp), the inherited tokenization tax is reduced by 59-81x for k-mer (NTv2), 300-800x for character-level (HyenaDNA, Caduceus, Evo2, NTv3), and 1.2-3.4x for BPE (DNABERT-2, GENA-LM, METAGENE-1) — the BPE win scaling with the severity of the underlying CPU-side bottleneck. On 32-kbp ultra-long sequences, character-level speedups reach >2500x for NTv3 and >1000x for Evo2. Bit-identical correctness was verified by token-by-token comparison against the reference implementation on every hardware platform.

Fit and differentiation. The contribution combines a substantive methods component — a unified framework spanning a diverse model zoo with strict correctness guarantees — with a conceptual reframing of how genomic tokenization should be designed. Existing GPU-tokenization work targets language models and does not address the genomic case; existing genomic foundation-model papers adopt NLP tokenizers without revisiting the assumption. We propose to release the implementation as an open library, drop-in compatible with the existing model ecosystem, requiring no retraining.

I would be grateful for a brief signal of fit before formal submission. The manuscript and figures are complete.
